# Design Basis & Methodology

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this self-directed portfolio project is to demonstrate a conceptual land-development workflow integrating site grading, storm servicing, stormwater management, calculations, and Civil 3D plan/profile production.

The subject site is approximately 0.115 ha and is located at 187 Borden Avenue North, Kitchener, Ontario.

## 2. Existing Conditions

The existing site is represented by residential buildings, driveways/hardscape, and landscaped pervious areas. Existing municipal infrastructure is present in Borden Avenue North and East Avenue, including water, sanitary, and storm services.

The pre-development drainage plan was used to establish the existing land-use distribution, composite runoff coefficient, and conceptual drainage direction.

## 3. Proposed Development

The proposed concept consists of three-storey stacked townhouse units, an access from Borden Avenue North, on-site parking, and associated servicing, grading, and drainage works.

The proposed land-use condition increases impervious coverage and therefore increases the site's runoff response.

## 4. Proposed Grading

A conceptual finished-grade surface was developed in Civil 3D to:

- provide positive drainage away from the proposed building;
- direct runoff toward proposed catchbasins and designated surface routes;
- establish ridge and valley controls;
- provide localized low points for temporary surface ponding; and
- coordinate minor-system collection with major-system overland drainage.

Civil 3D Water Drop analysis and user-defined contours were used as review tools to assess surface-flow direction and localized ponding limits.

The conceptual maximum ponding depth used in the project is approximately 0.20 m.

## 5. Hydrologic Assessment

Peak runoff is calculated using the Rational Method:

`Q = 2.78 C I A`

where:

- `Q` = peak runoff rate, L/s
- `C` = weighted runoff coefficient
- `I` = rainfall intensity, mm/hr
- `A` = drainage area, ha

A conceptual time of concentration of 10 minutes is used. Rainfall intensities are based on City of Kitchener IDF parameters contained in the project calculation workbook/report.

The conceptual quantity-control objective is:

`Qpost,100-year <= Qpre,5-year`

The report identifies the pre-development 5-year peak flow as approximately 16.28 L/s and the uncontrolled post-development 100-year peak flow as approximately 49.14 L/s.

## 6. Quantity Control

The project evaluates temporary on-site detention using a combination of:

- shallow surface ponding;
- storage within storm sewer pipes; and
- storage within drainage structures.

The Modified Rational Method is used to evaluate conceptual storage over a range of storm durations. The report identifies a critical preliminary storage requirement of approximately 25.16 m3 for the 60 mm theoretical trial outlet.

Detailed storage calculations indicate approximately:

- 17.84 m3 surface ponding storage;
- 3.17 m3 storm sewer pipe storage; and
- 7.03 m3 drainage-structure storage.

This provides approximately 28.02 m3 of total available conceptual storage.

## 7. Outlet Control

The orifice equation used is:

`Q = Cd A sqrt(2 g h)`

with a discharge coefficient of 0.8 in the project calculation.

A 60 mm theoretical trial opening produces approximately 15.35 L/s under the assumed head, which is below the 16.28 L/s allowable release. The report also notes a 75 mm minimum orifice criterion. A 75 mm opening under the same assumed head would exceed the allowable release, so the final outlet-control arrangement requires additional design iteration.

This project therefore treats the 60 mm result as a hydraulic trial rather than a final approved outlet-control design.

## 8. Minor Storm Sewer System

The minor system consists of catchbasins/drainage structures connected by storm sewer pipes and routed toward the downstream outlet and municipal connection.

Civil 3D Pipe Network tools were used to coordinate:

- structure locations and rim elevations;
- pipe diameters and slopes;
- invert elevations;
- available cover;
- structure depths; and
- the downstream connection condition.

The network was reviewed in both plan and profile.

## 9. Major Drainage System

The finished-grade surface provides a conceptual overland drainage route for runoff that exceeds the minor-system capacity or cannot immediately enter the catchbasins.

Detailed design would require verification that emergency overflow is safely conveyed without unacceptable impacts to buildings, adjacent properties, or public infrastructure.

## 10. Stormwater Quality

An oil-grit separator (OGS) is conceptually proposed downstream of the private storm sewer system and upstream of the municipal storm sewer connection.

No proprietary unit is finalized. Detailed design would require unit selection and sizing based on tributary drainage area, imperviousness, treatment target, hydraulic conditions, inlet/outlet elevations, and verified performance data.

## 11. Limitations

This portfolio project is not an issued design. It does not replace survey, utility verification, downstream capacity assessment, detailed hydraulic modelling, current regulatory/municipal criteria confirmation, geotechnical investigation, or professional engineering review.
